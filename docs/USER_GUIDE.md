# User Guide

This guide covers the screens, controls, and features of **WiiMC-SS XE**.

---

## Main Interface

The top navigation bar provides access to five primary media sections:

* **Videos:** Browse video files stored on SD cards, USB drives, or network shares.
* **Music:** Browse audio files, playlists, and game audio archives.
* **Online Media (Radio):** Stream internet radio and SHOUTcast feeds with dynamic live metadata.
* **DVD:** Play physical DVD-Video discs directly from the Wii optical drive.
* **Settings:** Configure audio, video, subtitle, network, and XE switching options.

---

## Videos

When browsing video folders:
* **Tiled 720x480p Playback:** Provides sharp, full-resolution playback on progressive-scan displays.
* **Artwork Viewer:** Place an image named `folder.jpg` or `[videoname].jpg` alongside your video to display high-resolution cover art.
* **Descriptions:** An accompanying `.xml` file containing synopsis information will display in the info sidebar.
* **Shuffle & Loop:** Cycle playback through continuous, shuffle, or single-file loop modes via the on-screen action bar.

---

## Music

The music player is built for deep album and soundtrack listening:
* **Embedded Cover Art:** Supports embedded ID3 and container art in MP3, AAC, ALAC, FLAC, OGG, WMA, and AIFF.
* **Game Audio Formats:** Full support for looping ADX and BRSTM files, respecting game loop points seamlessly.
* **Background Play:** Browse files or photos while music continues playing uninterrupted.

---

## Online Media (Radio)

* **Radio Directory:** Pre-loaded with popular internet radio stations.
* **Dynamic Art:** Updates station art and song titles automatically from providers like TuneIn, Yggdrasil Radio, and ANISON.FM.
* **Auto-Reconnect:** If a radio stream temporarily drops or hiccups, WiiMC-SS XE automatically reconnects after 60 seconds.

---

## DVD

Insert a standard DVD-Video into your Wii console and select the **DVD** icon from the top bar. Use the pointer or D-pad to navigate DVD menus, select titles and chapters, and switch audio tracks or subtitles.

> Note: Newer Wii consoles (manufactured after late 2009) and the Wii U lack the DVD-drive controller chip required to read commercial DVD-Video discs.
